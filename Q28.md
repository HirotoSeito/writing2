# <span style="color:red">Some people think that a huge amount of time and money is spent on the protection of wild animals, and that this money could be better spent on the human population.  To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion?</span>
Many individuals argue that the protection of wild animals should not require as much time and public money, and it is more worthy to invest them in society. Personally, I totally disagree with this statement as wildlife takes a lot of time to restore and benefits human in different ways. 
<br>
One compelling reason why I agree with this view is that human may greatly take advantage of wild animals. These natural creatures often play a key role in maintaining food chain, which balances the ecosystem. Without protection, food crisis will be more likely to occur in many countries, especially underdeveloped ones, since they go extinction. In addition, it is clear that those animals help researchers find potential cures for specific diseases. It is scientifically proven that substances from snakes are able to be used as a poison-proof medication or painkiller.
<br>
Another certain reason that I disapprove of this opinion is the possibility of restoring those animals. Once becoming extinct, many species cannot be restored with any methods or measures, which may lead to biodiversity. With respect to human society, governments can pour investments into multiple fields such as healthcare and education to develop their countries. Therefore, government budget spent on animal protection is never redundant and should be viewed as a long-term investment in human well-being rather than a waste of money. 
<br>
In conclusion, while some argue that investments in human population is more essential, I agree that the protection of wild animals should deserve the expenditure of time and funding because of benefits provided by them.
